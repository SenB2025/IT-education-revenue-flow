CASE STUDY 02 · B2C TRAINING BUSINESS

Can a specialist training academy make each cohort commercially viable?

A small academy offers a 10-week applied technology programme. It must balance learner fees against instructor payments, marketing, lab access, learner support and the cost of keeping the business operational between cohorts.

BREAK-EVEN THINKING

Enrolment is the critical variable

At a fee of ₹18,000 per learner, a 30-person cohort generates ₹5.4 lakh. If the academy has fixed cohort commitments, a fall in enrolment can quickly weaken the economics.

LOW ENROLMENT

15 learners

Gross revenue falls to ₹2.7 lakh. The academy must assess whether it should run, defer, merge or cancel the cohort.

PLANNED COHORT

30 learners

Gross revenue reaches ₹5.4 lakh. The planned budget can be tested against actual delivery and acquisition costs.

HIGHER VOLUME

45 learners

Gross revenue reaches ₹8.1 lakh, but additional trainer capacity and support may be required; costs do not remain flat indefinitely.

BUSINESS LESSON

Revenue growth without cost discipline can be misleading

A training academy needs a repeatable enrolment engine, a minimum viable cohort threshold, disciplined marketing spend, effective trainer utilisation and evidence of learner outcomes that supports referrals and repeat purchases.
