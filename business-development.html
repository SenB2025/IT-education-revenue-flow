FROM PROSPECT TO PARTNERSHIP

A disciplined business development process for education

Business development in education is relationship-led, but it should not be informal or unmeasured. A repeatable process helps turn conversations with institutions, employers and public agencies into qualified opportunities, proposals and sustainable contracts.

01

Map the market

Identify target sectors, institutions, employers, government programmes and geographic clusters. Prioritise organisations with a relevant need and plausible budget.

Output: target account list and stakeholder map
02

Understand the buyer

Establish the problem, decision-maker, users, procurement route, budget cycle, success criteria and competing options.

Output: discovery notes and qualification assessment
03

Shape the proposition

Connect the buyer's need to a specific programme or service. Define outcomes, delivery approach, scope, timeline, price and responsibilities.

Output: concept note or solution brief
04

Build the commercial case

Estimate revenue, delivery costs, partner payments, acquisition costs, cash-flow timing and operational risks. Avoid pricing solely by copying competitors.

Output: costing sheet and proposal
05

Negotiate and formalise

Agree deliverables, service levels, payment milestones, intellectual property, data handling, cancellation terms, reporting and renewal conditions.

Output: approved contract or memorandum of understanding
06

Deliver, review and grow

Track performance, resolve issues, document outcomes and identify repeat cohorts, additional departments or adjacent programmes.

Output: review report, renewal plan and account growth opportunity

PARTNERSHIP HEALTH CHECK

What makes a relationship commercially durable?

For the customer

  • Clear outcomes and measurable value
  • Reliable delivery and responsive support
  • Transparent pricing and reporting
  • Confidence in quality and reputation

For the provider

  • Realistic scope and delivery commitments
  • Timely payment and manageable cash flow
  • Access to decision-makers and users
  • Potential for renewal and account growth
BUSINESS LESSON

Relationships create access; execution creates repeat business

A strong business development function connects market understanding, stakeholder engagement, commercial judgement and operational follow-through. The signed agreement is the start of delivery, not the end of the sales process.
