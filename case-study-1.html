CASE STUDY 01 · B2B2C / CO-DELIVERY

A university–industry professional training partnership

A university wants to offer an industry-relevant digital skills programme but does not have all the specialist delivery capacity in-house. A training partner brings curriculum, trainers and delivery operations; an employer contributes skills requirements and access to potential learners.

01 / THE NEED

University

Expand its continuing education portfolio, strengthen employer relationships and create a new income stream without building a complete delivery team.

02 / THE PARTNER

Training provider

Supply specialist instructors, labs, learning materials, learner support and programme coordination under an agreed service contract.

03 / THE MARKET

Employer & learners

Employers help define relevant competencies; individual learners or sponsoring organisations pay for the programme.

COMMERCIAL DESIGN

What needs to be agreed before launch?

Contract and operating questions

  • Who owns the learner relationship and enrolment data?
  • Who sets pricing, discounts and refund rules?
  • Which party is responsible for marketing and lead conversion?
  • Who controls academic quality, assessment and certification?
  • What happens if enrolment falls below the minimum viable cohort?

Measures of success

  • Qualified leads and enquiry-to-enrolment conversion
  • Enrolment against the minimum cohort size
  • Completion, learner satisfaction and employer feedback
  • Cost per enrolled learner and contribution per cohort
  • Repeat business and additional programme demand
BUSINESS LESSON

A partnership is not simply a revenue-sharing agreement

It is an operating model. Commercial viability depends on clearly assigned responsibilities, a reliable route to market, delivery quality, transparent unit economics and a contract that allocates both income and risk.
