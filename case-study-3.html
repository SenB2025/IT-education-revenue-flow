CASE STUDY 03 · CROSS-BORDER PARTNERSHIP

Taking a professional education programme into a new country

An education provider based in Country A wants to enter Country B. Rather than immediately establishing a full local operation, it explores a delivery partnership with a local institution or training company.

HOME-MARKET PROVIDER

Programme owner

Provides curriculum, brand, specialist faculty, quality standards and programme oversight.

LOCAL PARTNER

Market operator

Provides local market knowledge, institutional relationships, learner recruitment, facilities and local coordination.

SUPPORTING PARTNERS

Technology & employers

Provide digital platforms, assessment tools, local industry input, internships or employer recognition where agreed.

MARKET ENTRY OPTIONS

Three possible operating structures

01

Licensing

The local partner pays a licence or royalty to use approved content, curriculum or brand assets. The provider needs strong quality assurance and brand protection.

02

Co-delivery

Both parties contribute to programme delivery and share revenue or defined service fees. Responsibilities and learner ownership must be explicit.

03

Local delivery contract

The provider contracts a local organisation to recruit learners or deliver defined services for a fixed fee or performance-linked payment.

ILLUSTRATIVE REVENUE FLOW

A simple co-delivery example

Assume 80 learners pay the equivalent of ₹25,000 each. Total programme revenue is ₹20,00,000 before local taxes, refunds and other adjustments.

Programme owner: ₹9,00,000 (45%)Local partner: ₹7,00,000 (35%)Delivery reserve / contribution: ₹4,00,000 (20%)
Illustrative model only. A real agreement must specify currency, exchange-rate treatment, tax withholding, payment timing, refunds, enrolment targets and which party bears each cost.
DUE DILIGENCE BEFORE ENTRY

Questions that reduce commercial and operational risk

Market and regulatory

  • Is the programme permitted under local education and business regulations?
  • Are local accreditation or recognition requirements applicable?
  • What is the demand, competitive landscape and realistic price point?
  • What currency, tax and remittance rules affect payments?

Partner and delivery

  • Can the partner demonstrate recruitment and delivery capability?
  • Who owns learner data and handles complaints?
  • How will faculty quality and assessment standards be monitored?
  • What are the exit, renewal and dispute-resolution provisions?
BUSINESS LESSON

International expansion is a partnership and risk-management decision

Local knowledge can reduce the cost and uncertainty of market entry, but only when the partner's role, incentives, authority, service standards and accountability are clearly defined.
